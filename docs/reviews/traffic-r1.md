# traffic — round 1 critic review

**Score: 6.2 / 10 — FAIL** (perf and correctness are strong; the module's own signature camera preset undermines the visual judgment criterion it's meant to prove)

## Perf & errors
- 37 valid captures (default/night/intersection variants × 4 tods × 3 presets, plus extra probes): **minFps 116.8, maxGpuMs 6.5ms** (budget ≤20ms — comfortably inside), **maxDrawCalls 103** (whole showcase scene: terrain+environment+roads+traffic+effects).
- Traffic's **own** draw call count independently verified fixed at **exactly 18 InstancedMesh** (`scene.getObjectByName('traffic').traverse()`) at both 42 active cars and 93 active cars (max for the demo grid) — confirms the "fixed 18 regardless of car count" claim precisely.
- `update()` cost independently timed via direct `module.update(1/60, ctx)` calls: **0.019ms/call at 93 active cars** (200-call average). Couldn't reach exactly 300 cars (this demo grid's lane length caps `maxCars` at 93; `MAX_POOL`=300 is a separate global ceiling), but the measured per-car cost extrapolates to ~0.06ms at 300 — consistent with, and under, the builder's ~0.1ms claim.
- **Console errors: 0** in all 39 attempted captures. **Math.random: 0 hits** in `src/traffic/`. Folder contains only its own 5 files.
- One flaky capture: `shots/traffic/r1c-int/traffic_closeup-21h5.png` came back as a black frame (0 draw calls, fps=-2261, `roads` reported as `"loaded"` not `"ok"`, `traffic` missing from `modules`) on the first attempt, reproduced once more, then succeeded cleanly twice in a row (96 draw calls, 3.74ms gpu, 0 errors). This reads as dev-server/navigation-timing flakiness in the shot tool rather than a traffic defect — flagging for the record but not scoring against the module.

## Ranked issues

1. **The `traffic:closeup` preset is effectively broken as a "look at the cars" shot — a vehicle fills most of the frame as a flat, undetailed box, in every single capture.** Where: `shots/traffic/r1c/traffic_closeup-6h5.png`, `-12.png`, `-18.png`, `-21h5.png` (and identically in `shots/traffic/r2/traffic_closeup-12.png`, the exact reference PNG named in the brief, and again in `shots/traffic/r1c-night/traffic_closeup-21h5.png`). At every time-of-day and in both variants tested, the fixed camera position (`traffic:closeup` in `src/traffic/index.js`, pos `[b.cx-20, y+7, b.cz+12]`) sits close enough to a lane that a car (usually the same blue/white van or bus given the deterministic seed) is parked or queued almost directly in front of it, filling ~40% of the frame with a shapeless colored slab — no visible cabin glass, no lights, no bevel detail, because the camera is too close and too low relative to the vehicle's near face. This directly undercuts judge criterion (a): the one preset built to show off the car models instead hides them. Fix: pull `traffic:closeup` back and/or raise it further off any lane centerline (the in-code comment claims "well above and back from any lane, so it can never end up nose-to-tail with a car," which only guards against longitudinal alignment, not lateral proximity — a car can still end up beside/under the camera). Simplest robust fix: bias the target/position away from the nearest lane polyline by a minimum clearance (e.g. ≥8m), or pick a fixed offset from a lane midpoint on an arm with no signal queue nearby.

2. **Cars have no visible wheels.** Where: my own extreme closeup (`side_closeup_day.png`/`extreme_closeup_day.png`, custom camera 3-4m from an isolated sedan) — confirmed directly in `src/traffic/models.js` `wheelArchGeometry()`: "Dark side skirts near the bottom (reads as wheel arches without animating actual wheels)." From any distance closer than the standard presets, cars read as a rounded box floating over a flat black stripe, with zero wheel/tire cylinders. This is a real gap against the reference rubric's "4–6-stud-wide Blox cars" identity and hurts assembly credibility at anything but overview distance. Fix: add a cheap instanced wheel cylinder (2 or 4 per car, shared geometry) even if non-rotating — it's what actually reads as "wheel arch" versus a stripe.

3. **Glass cabin reads as a flat, semi-opaque box rather than glossy/transmissive glass.** Where: `extreme_closeup_day.png`. `M.glass('transClear', { opacity: 0.5 })` gives a uniform translucent slab with no window-pillar breakup, no stronger specular response than the body plastic, and it's barely distinguishable as "glass" versus tinted plastic at close range. Minor next to issue #1/#2, but part of the same "doesn't hold up close" pattern. Fix: bump clearcoat/roughness contrast on the glass material relative to body, or add a thin pillar strip to break up the box.

4. **Round-robin-by-arm signal design measurably reduces throughput and produces visible queuing**, by design (documented in `lanegraph.js` comments) — verified structurally correct (every arm gets a fixed, starvation-free turn; `GREEN_S=12, ALLRED_S=2`, `ENTRY_MARGIN_S=5` correctly leaves margin so a late entrant can clear before the next arm's window). Where: `shots/traffic/r1c/traffic_default-18.png` and `shots/traffic/r1c-night/traffic_default-21h5.png` both show a real 4-6 car backup on one arm while the others sit empty. At the busiest (4-way) intersection the cycle is `4×(12+2)=56s`, so a given arm only gets ~12.5% real entry time. Independently verified via `getVehiclePositions()` + an OBB overlap check (below) that this design achieves its stated goal (no crossing-path collisions), but the throughput cost is real and visible, not just theoretical — worth a throughput tune (shorter ALLRED, or protected-left add-on for low-conflict pairs) in a future pass if "living, flowing city" is a priority over strict simplicity.
   - Overlap check methodology: sampled `api.getVehiclePositions()` every 0.1s of sim time over 40 frames (~4s), box-tested every pair within 12m using a uniform van-sized OBB (5.3×2.0m, since the public API doesn't expose per-car spec/size) via separating-axis test. Result: **844 flagged "overlaps," all 844 same-heading (±20°) pairs** — i.e. normal close-following, a false-positive artifact of using one uniform box for all three car sizes — **and 0 cross-heading (20°–160°) overlaps**, which is what would indicate real clipping between conflicting paths. A second, more conservative pass using the smallest real body box (sedan, 4.3×1.85m) for every car found **0 overlaps of any kind across 1291 pairs over 6 samples spaced 1.5s apart**. Both checks corroborate the builder's "zero OBB overlaps" claim.

5. **Non-braking taillight emissive (3.2) is below the ARCHITECTURE §7 red-emissive bloom threshold (≥4).** Verified directly by reading live material `emissiveIntensity` via `page.evaluate`: `headNight`=3.5 (white, bloom-eligible per the 2.5–3 threshold), `tailNormalNight`=3.2 (red, **not** bloom-eligible), `tailBrakeNight`=5.0 (red, bloom-eligible, correctly brighter than normal). So braking cars do correctly pop brighter at night (confirmed both in material values and visually in `shots/traffic/r1c-night/traffic_intersection-21h5.png`), but a moving car's ordinary taillights won't bloom, reading slightly duller/less "alive" than headlights in the same shot. Minor; likely intentional (keeps braking as the salient night cue) but worth a deliberate call either way.
   - Also noted: `car.braking` goes false the instant a car reaches a full stop (diff back to 0), so a car parked at a red light for several seconds shows the dim, non-bloom taillight rather than staying on the bright brake material the whole time it's stopped — a small deviation from how real brake lights behave, though not something a casual screenshot viewer would catch.

## What already works well
- **Model variety and readability at normal/overview range** are good: three genuinely distinct silhouettes (sedan/van/bus, 4.3/5.3/9.4m), 13-color palette, visible clearcoat specular highlights, correct scale relative to lane/crosswalk width — see `shots/traffic/r1c/traffic_default-6h5.png` and `traffic_intersection-12.png`.
- **Night lighting is convincing**: round headlight/taillight elements read clearly with correct bloom, distinguishable colors (white vs. red), and the round-robin queuing itself becomes a nice visual (one arm flowing through a stopped queue) — see `shots/traffic/r1c-night/traffic_intersection-21h5.png`.
- **Simulation engineering is solid and matches its own claims**: fixed 18 draw calls independent of car count, cheap update cost, deterministic (no `Math.random`), zero console errors, and a signal model that is verifiably conflict-free (0 cross-direction OBB overlaps across a real independent check) even though its throughput tradeoff is visible.
