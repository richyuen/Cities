## Ranked issues (most damaging first)

1. **Corner pilasters (and any other same-plane accent) are perfectly flush with the wall — zero physical relief, reads as a painted stripe, not a proud Lego brick.** `generateCommercial`'s level-3 office tower draws 4 corner "pilasters" (`generator.js` ~line 270) centered at `cx + sx*(halfW - pw/2)`, i.e. their outer face lands exactly on the main body box's own wall face (`cx + sx*halfW`) — genuinely coincident geometry, not just visually close. Confirmed in `shots/buildings/r1-custom/corner-pilaster-closeup.png` and `roof-angled-office.png`: the accent colour reads as a flat 2D seam with no bevel highlight or AO, unlike every *horizontal* band in the same file (roof caps, awnings, setback accents), which all correctly use the `*1.01`/`*1.02` outward-scale trick to avoid exactly this. This is the tallest, most-showcased building type (`tower-office`, always the `closeupTarget`) and is exactly what `shots/buildings/dev-2.png` puts in the viewer's face — it's the single most damaging miss against rubric item (a), "genuinely made of Lego bricks." **Fix:** give the pilaster the same outward-inflation treatment already used elsewhere (e.g. `pw*1.15` or an explicit +0.05–0.08m outward offset) so it visibly overhangs the wall and catches a highlight/shadow like the rest of the trim.

2. **Camera can end up inside a building's hollow interior, where backface-culled walls vanish and you see straight through to the sky/terrain between window-frame strips.** Reproduced with a custom camera placed inside the office tower's footprint (`shots/buildings/r1-custom/corner-pilaster-closeup.png`, `corner-pilaster-extreme.png` — camera at `cx+2.2..5, height*0.3, cz+2.2..5`, well inside the ~7m footprint half-width). Buildings are single-shell boxes (front-face-only `MeshPhysicalMaterial`), so from inside, every exterior wall face is a backface and gets culled — only the inward-facing sides of the small window-frame boxes remain visible as isolated dark rectangles floating in front of open sky. `core/camera.js` sets `controls.minDistance = 4` with no XZ collision against building volumes, so this is reachable by ordinary orbit/pan around a tower cluster, not just an artificial camera. Lower priority than #1 because it needs an unusual camera path to trigger, but it's a real, demonstrable geometry bug of exactly the kind the brief asked to hunt for. **Fix:** either give the body box a cheap `THREE.DoubleSide` (or a dark inward-facing cap) so the shell reads as solid from any angle, or clamp orbit targets away from building AABBs at the camera-control level.

3. **Office/commercial glass-tower facades read flatter and less "Lego" than residential facades — the module's fidelity is inconsistent by zone, and the weaker one is the one shown closest.** Compare `shots/buildings/r1-custom/roof-angled-resi.png` and `house-closeup.png` (residential: strong layered frame→bezel→pane relief with visible AO/highlight, nicely bevelled roof cap and roof-mounted tank) against `dev-2.png` / `buildings_closeup-12.png` / `buildings_closeup-18.png` (office tower: thin, low-contrast frame outline, no strong highlight, closer to "glass curtain wall with punched panels" than a Lego window brick). Since the tallest building in every `default`/`closeup` showcase is always this office-tower type, the module's single most-photographed close-up is its weakest-looking one — this is precisely the ambiguity the brief flagged about `dev-2.png`. **Fix:** bring office-tower window frame width/protrusion and lighting contrast up to the residential facade's level, or add a sill/lintel like the balcony ledges already used on residential level 2.

4. *(minor)* **Daytime "lit" window overlay renders as a solid black square, not tinted glass.** `winWarm`/`winCool` are `plastic('black', { emissive: ..., emissiveIntensity: 0 })` by day, so the ~40–58% of windows randomly chosen as "lit" (independent of time of day) show as flat black rectangles sitting proud of the lighter glass pane at noon (clearly visible in `buildings_closeup-12.png`/`-18.png`). It doesn't flicker or z-fight (offsets are correctly staggered), and can be read as "blinds down," but at close range it looks like scattered blacked-out windows rather than deliberate variation. Cheap fix: give the base (unlit) material a light tint instead of pure black, or skip the overlay box entirely when `emissiveIntensity` is 0 and just vary the glass pane colour instead.

5. *(minor, likely not buildings' fault)* Window glass uses opacity-based transparency (`materials.glass()` defaults `transmission: 0`), not true transmissive plastic as `REFERENCE.md` calls for. This is a `core/materials.js` default, not something `buildings` overrides — flagging for awareness, not counted against this module's score.

6. *(minor)* Aviation beacon (`generator.js` — `beacon` material, red emissive block atop tall towers/antennas) is present and does light up at night (visible as a tiny red pixel in `buildings_default-21h5.png` and `buildings_levels-21h5.png`), but it's extremely subtle — a couple of pixels even at showcase-close range. Given the rubric explicitly calls this out, it deserves a bit more presence (bigger block, or a stronger bloom-friendly intensity).

## What already works
- **Genuine building-type variety, not scaled copies.** The 9 zone×level grid (`shots/buildings/dev-3-levels.png`, `shots/buildings/r1-levels/*`) shows real massing differences: house → apartment-with-balconies → setback residential tower with antenna+beacon; single-storey shop-with-awning → mid-rise office → curtain-wall tower with corner pilasters; low corrugated warehouse → taller warehouse → plant with twin chimneys, storage tank and a connecting pipe. Each kind reads distinctly at a glance, which is the right answer to rubric item (b).
- **Night lighting is convincing.** `buildings_default-21h5.png` / `buildings_closeup-21h5.png` show a good warm/cool window mix with plausible randomly-dark windows (not a uniform grid), a clearly visible glowing-orange chimney on an industrial roof, and roof studs + rooftop mechanical units reading nicely with real bevels/highlights from an angled view (`r1-custom/roof-angled-resi.png`, `roof-angled-office.png`).
- **Solid engineering underneath**: zero console errors anywhere, no RNG determinism violations, a batching scheme that's been independently stress-tested at ~10x showcase scale and still amortizes draw calls correctly, and an API surface that matches its documented contract exactly.
